How Much Does a Website Cost in 2026? A Clear Breakdown

By M. Bilal · 8 min read · Updated August 2026

It is the first question almost everyone asks, and the honest answer is: it depends. A simple website can cost a few hundred, while a large custom platform can run into five figures. That range is not evasive, it reflects the fact that a website is built to a spec, like a house. This guide breaks down what actually moves the price, so you can budget with confidence and avoid overpaying or underbuying.

What actually drives the price

Two websites that look similar on the surface can differ wildly in cost. These are the factors that matter most:

  • Number of pages. A five-page brochure site is far cheaper than a fifty-page site with location and service pages.
  • Custom design vs template. A unique, conversion-focused design costs more than a lightly customized theme, and usually earns it back.
  • Functionality. Contact forms are simple. Bookings, memberships, dashboards, and payments add real development time.
  • eCommerce. Product catalogs, variants, checkout, and shipping rules are their own project.
  • Content and SEO. Copywriting, photography, and technical SEO setup take time, and they are what make a site perform.

Typical price ranges in 2026

These ranges reflect professional, custom work, not do-it-yourself builders. Use them as a starting point, not a final quote.

Simple marketing website

A clean, mobile-first site of up to eight pages with a contact form and basic SEO usually falls between $800 and $2,500. Ideal for local businesses and service providers who need to look credible and capture leads.

Business or custom website

Bespoke design, more pages, a blog, and integrations such as a CRM or booking tool typically land between $2,500 and $8,000. This is the sweet spot for companies that treat their website as a real sales channel.

eCommerce store

A custom Shopify or WooCommerce store with optimized product pages and checkout generally runs $1,500 to $6,000+, depending on catalog size and the apps you need.

Web app or custom platform

Portals, dashboards, and software-style products are quoted per project and often start around $8,000 and climb from there based on scope.

One-time cost vs ongoing cost

The build is a one-time investment. Keeping a site healthy has smaller recurring costs you should plan for:

  • Domain: around $10 to $20 per year.
  • Hosting: a few dollars a month on shared hosting, more for high-traffic apps.
  • Maintenance: optional updates, backups, and security, often a small monthly plan.
  • SEO: if you want to rank and grow, an ongoing retainer compounds over time.

How to get the most value

The cheapest site is rarely the best value. A slightly larger investment in strategy, speed, and SEO usually pays for itself in leads. Be clear about your goals, ask for a fixed scope and timeline, and make sure you own your site, hosting, and accounts at the end. That is how you avoid surprise costs and lock-in.
